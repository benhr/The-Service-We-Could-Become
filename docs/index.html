The Service We Could Become

The Service We Could Become

A short text game for PHCM9391 Health Organisations, Strategy and Change. You play the first chief executive of a new integrated care organisation in a fictional Australian region. It plans and funds services, but it can't direct most of the people who deliver them.

Working prototype for the research team, November 2026. The text is a first draft and the names are working names.

Scenes involving the Aboriginal Medical Service are a first draft, to be reviewed by an Aboriginal colleague.

The game collects no data. Your choices stay in your browser.

Decisions made with partners you can't direct

Each episode puts you in a situation drawn from that week's topic and asks you to decide something with incomplete information, limited time and partners who have their own interests. There is no right answer. What you decide changes what happens next, in the episode and in later ones: who will work with you, what money and staff you have, and which options are still open.

Episodes take about 15 minutes. They are optional and not assessed, and nothing in tutorials depends on having played. Each one ends with questions to bring to your tutorial.

At the end of each episode you get a save code. Enter it at the start of the next episode and the story picks up from your decisions. Without a code, an episode starts from a typical position.

The Tallowfield Integrated Care Authority

The game is set in Tallowfield, a fictional region of about 880,000 people. After new state and federal governments agreed a new National Health Reform Agreement, Integrated Care Authorities are being set up across the country. Each one takes over the planning and funding of its region's community health services, primary care commissioning and programs meant to keep people out of hospital.

Work that two organisations did for two governments, the Local Health District for the state and the Primary Health Network for the Commonwealth, now sits with one new body. The Tallowfield Authority starts with a pooled budget of about $475 million a year, $410 million of it moved from the Local Health District and $65 million from the Primary Health Network, plus $34 million to run itself. About 140 staff have come across from the two organisations, along with hundreds of contracts and programs.

The Authority's reach stops well short of the whole system. The hospitals stay with the Local Health District. General practices and private allied health are independent businesses, funded mostly through Medicare. Aged care, disability services and social care are outside its remit, although they shape most of the problems it is meant to solve. The law that created it will be reviewed in three years, and the Minister's office is watching.

You play the Authority's first chief executive, starting in its first week.

The region, the Authority and everyone in it are fictional. The funding arrangements are modelled on Australian ones.
